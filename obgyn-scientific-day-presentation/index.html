Chapter 03 · The disease

A pregnancy implanted inside the scar itself.

A caesarean scar ectopic is a uterine ectopic pregnancy: the blastocyst implants inside the myometrial defect - the niche - left behind by a previous caesarean.

Poor scar healing leaves a thin niche in the anterior wall

The blastocyst implants in the niche instead of the cavity

Scar tissue has no decidua - trophoblast invasion is unopposed

It burrows deep: the precursor of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS)

Risk factors: previous caesarean (risk climbs with each one) · scar niche · short interpregnancy interval · other uterine surgeries.

Chapter 04 · The diagnosis

Transvaginal ultrasound makes the call.

No laparotomy, no MRI first - a probe, a systematic eye, and seven signs. The aim is to catch it before nine weeks, when every option is still on the table.

  • Empty uterine cavity and empty cervical canal
  • Sac low and anterior - at the level of the scar
  • Triangular or oval sac moulding into the niche
  • Thin or absent myometrium toward the bladder
  • Negative “sliding sign” under gentle probe pressure
  • High-velocity, low-resistance flow around the sac
  • ± Yolk sac or embryo with cardiac activity

Chapter 05 · Two patterns

On the scar, or in the niche - very different features.

Chapter 06 · The rules

The guidelines are unambiguous.

SMFM · Consult Series #63 - 2022

Recommends against expectant management of CSEP with cardiac activity. First-trimester termination is advised once diagnosed.

RCOG · Green-top Guideline No. 21 - 2016

Counsel on severe maternal morbidity and mortality. No single best intervention, but surgical approaches are the most effective.

Our patient heard exactly this - twice. She still chose to continue.

The question the guidelines don't answer

…and what if we continue?

Chapter 08 · Her pregnancy, week by week

She followed up. This is how it unfolded.

Serial imaging told a very predictable story - the natural history of the evidence you just read, happening on schedule. The difference: it was watched, anticipated, and planned for.

  1. Week 6+

    CSEP diagnosed

    Live embryo in the scar niche. Termination recommended; she continues after written informed consent.

  2. Week 11+

    The placenta shows its hand

    NT scan normal - but the anterior-wall placenta covers the internal os.

  3. Week 22+

    Previa, and vascularity

    Previa extending from anterior to posterior wall, with marked isthmic vascularity.

  4. Week 26

    MRI confirms the fear

    Ultrasound shows a thin lower segment; MRI shows complete placenta previa, accreta, and a focal hematoma.

  5. Weeks 31–34

    Invasion confirmed

    Ultrasound and MRI together confirm invasive implantation. Nothing here is a surprise anymore.

  6. Week 35

    Admitted

    The multidisciplinary team assembles and plans a caesarean hysterectomy - on their terms, not the emergency's.

Chapter 09 · The delivery

35 weeks - a planned caesarean hysterectomy.

Rehearsed like a launch. The single most important decision: nobody would try to remove the placenta.

  1. Abdominal aortic balloon preset - inflated before hysterotomy

  2. Classical (upper-segment) caesarean under GA - baby delivered

  3. No attempt to remove the placenta - left in situ, uterus closed

  4. Total hysterectomy was done

One patient, one room, seven teams OBGYN surgery · urosurgery · anaesthesia · ICU · radiology · nursing · blood bank

Outcome

  • Blood loss 1690 mL - controlled (4 U RBC, plasma, fibrinogen)
  • ICU day 1 → ward day 2 → home within days
  • Pathology: percreta - an 8 × 6 cm penetrating area
  • Mother and baby both did well

Chapter 10 · The ethics

Three duties. None can be dropped.

i

Autonomy

Respect the informed mother's wish to keep her pregnancy.

ii

Non-maleficence

Prevent catastrophic rupture, haemorrhage, and maternal death.

iii

Informed consent

Transparent, objective risk data - without coercion, without abandonment.

The task is not choosing one - it is holding all three at once.

Respect for autonomy does not mean silent acquiescence to risk - and medical duty does not mean overriding a patient's choice.

It demands rigorous counselling, shared decision-making, and unwavering preparation to protect life.

Our highest duty is neither to compromise safety nor to dismiss hope - but to bridge diagnostic precision, meticulous counselling, and expert multidisciplinary care.
